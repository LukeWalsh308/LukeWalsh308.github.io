I am an applied economist working on education, labour and public economics, with a focus on how policy reforms shape individual outcomes such as human capital investment. I am a Research Associate at the University of Sheffield on an NIHR-funded project studying how health interventions affect employment and labour market outcomes. I carry out the causal analysis using bespoke administrative data that links outpatient records to high-frequency HMRC data. I also work part-time as a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at the University of Warwick on the ESRC-funded project “University-Level Study Abroad in Post-Brexit UK”. I completed my PhD in Economics at the University of Liverpool Management School in 2026.

My job market paper, “Delayed and Deterred: Tuition Fees, University Participation, and the Selectivity of Entrants”, studies the 2012 increase in tuition fees in England. Using the Longitudinal Education Outcomes (LEO) data, I compare young people born either side of the September school-entry cutoff before and after the reform, using a difference-in-discontinuities estimator I built for this setting. I find that higher fees led to a large fall in university attendance, concentrated in less selective universities. Despite the fall in attendance, I find no adverse effect on the labour market outcomes of the affected cohort. In a second paper, “Do Cuts to Higher Education Subsidies Reduce Economic Spillovers? Evidence from England’s Tuition Fee Reform”, I ask whether the same reform had wider costs for the economy. I find no detectable spillovers on innovation, output or the wages of non-graduates.
