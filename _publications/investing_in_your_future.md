title: "Delayed and Deterred: Tuition Fees, University
Participation, and the Selectivity of Entrants"
collection: publications
category: manuscripts
excerpt: '**Abstract:** How do students respond when university fees nearly triple, but nothing is paid upfront?
I examine England’s 2012 tuition fee reform using administrative data and a difference-in-
discontinuities design exploiting the September school-entry cutoff. On-time university entry
fell by 4.6 percentage points, with delayed entry offsetting roughly half the decline. Five-
year participation remained 2.2 points lower. Among entrants, attendance at above-mean and
top-quartile institutions rose by 6.0 and 5.3 points, respectively, but unconditional gains in
entry to selective universities across the full cohort were smaller. These patterns persist after
accounting for concurrent changes to student number controls. The apparent shift towards
more selective universities therefore largely reflects selection into higher education rather
than widespread upgrading.'


---
Increases in the cost of higher education raise concerns about the returns to human capital
and the extent to which financial barriers restrict access, yet little is known about their effects
in settings without credit constraints. This study leverages a natural experiment in which the
UK government substantially raised a cap on tuition fees, to estimate the causal impact on
educational attainment and labour market outcomes using a difference-in-discontinuities de-
sign. I find that the fee increase reduced university enrolment, particularly in fields with lower
expected returns, while students from low-income backgrounds were not disproportionately
affected, likely due to the UK’s income-contingent loan system which mitigates credit con-
straints. Overall, graduates affected by the reform were more likely to enter higher-paying
industries and transition into employment more quickly, while low-income graduates expe-
rienced a modest increase in earnings. These findings offer new insights into how higher
university costs influence human capital investment and post-graduation outcomes.
