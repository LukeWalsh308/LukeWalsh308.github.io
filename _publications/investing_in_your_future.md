How do students respond when university fees nearly triple, but nothing is paid upfront?
I examine England’s 2012 tuition fee reform using administrative data and a difference-in-
discontinuities design exploiting the September school-entry cutoff. On-time university entry
fell by 4.6 percentage points, with delayed entry offsetting roughly half the decline. Five-
year participation remained 2.2 points lower. Among entrants, attendance at above-mean and
top-quartile institutions rose by 6.0 and 5.3 points, respectively, but unconditional gains in
entry to selective universities across the full cohort were smaller. These patterns persist after
accounting for concurrent changes to student number controls. The apparent shift towards
more selective universities therefore largely reflects selection into higher education rather
than widespread upgrading.
